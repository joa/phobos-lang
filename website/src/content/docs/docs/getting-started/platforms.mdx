Phobos runs on NVIDIA GPUs of compute capability 7.5 or newer, Turing and
every architecture after it, on Windows x64 and Linux x64. Compiling a kernel
to PTX needs no GPU at all.

## Tested cards

Phobos is developed on an RTX 2080 SUPER, and every number in
[Benchmarks](/docs/reference/benchmarks/) was measured on it. It has also been
run and benchmarked on more cards, rented through vast.ai under Linux:

| Card | Architecture | Chip | Memory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RTX 2080 SUPER | Turing | `sm_75` | 8 GB |
| A40 | Ampere | `sm_86` | 48 GB |
| RTX 3090 | Ampere | `sm_86` | 24 GB |
| RTX 4060 | Ada | `sm_89` | 8 GB |
| RTX 4090 | Ada | `sm_89` | 24 GB |
| H100 PCIe | Hopper | `sm_90` | 80 GB |
| RTX 5090 | Blackwell | `sm_120` | 32 GB |

The tuning is done on the 2080 SUPER; a default that matters for speed is also
measured on a 3090 and a 4090 before it ships. The A40 is the data center
Ampere card the prompt kernels for large models were tuned on. The H100 runs
the kernels as compiled for its chip, untuned.

## Measured on each card

Every card ran the same two benchmarks on 2026-10-23, built from dbaa4f7.
The 3090 ran them later that day at c5f210f, which runs the same engine
code. Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M ran against llama.cpp b11681 through
`scripts/bench.py`: five rounds, the engines interleaved, three repetitions
each, the sizes of the [Benchmarks](/docs/reference/benchmarks/) run plus
prompts of 16384 and 32768 tokens. Then `phobos-kbench` ran twice against
cuBLAS in the same session, the first run autotuning and the second reusing its
winners, compiling the kernels for the card's own chip, which its JSON report
records, with 64-bit indices from `sm_80` on. The 2080 SUPER's model figures
are the ones [Benchmarks](/docs/reference/benchmarks/) publishes.

A ratio compares two engines on one card in one session. Rates on different
cards are not comparable to each other: the hosts differ, and so do the power
limits a host sets, though every rented card here ran at its stock limit. A rented card was checked for
another tenant before the run (idle power, utilization, and how long the
driver has spent capping its power or slowing the card for heat), and
`bench.py` checks it again between rounds. `bench.py` also compares the clock
under load with the card's maximum and flags a card the driver slowed for
heat. An H100 SXM ran at 64% of its maximum, slowed for heat in nearly every
sample, and was replaced by the H100 PCIe here; a 3090 at 70% was dropped the
same way. On the 3090 that check reads the card's own power state: it holds
about 150 W for two seconds after a process exits, at 0% utilization, and is
back at 21 W within ten
([trace](https://github.com/joa/phobos-lang/blob/main/results/platforms/rtx3090/power-decay.txt)).
Its run and the H100's therefore raised the check's power threshold from
120 W to 200 W; the utilization check was unchanged. The 4060 reports no power draw at all, so its
check was on utilization alone. Every round on every card came out
uncontended.

A release also carries compiled kernels for `sm_80`, the A100's chip. They
have not been tested or tuned on a card. Any other card of compute capability 7.5 or newer runs the
PTX for its chip, compiled on first use if the release's cache does not have
it (see [Kernel cache](/docs/inference/kernel-cache/)).

## Operating systems and drivers

- Windows x64, or Linux x64 with glibc 2.28 or newer.
- A driver from the CUDA 13 line, R580 or newer. Phobos emits PTX ISA 9.0,
  which older drivers refuse to load. An sm_120 card's kernels, compiled with libNVVM, are PTX ISA 9.3
  and need a driver that runs CUDA 13.3.

[Install](/docs/getting-started/install/) lists what a build from source needs
on each.

## What differs between chips

The language is the same on every chip, and a kernel compiles for any of them;
`phobos-compile --chip` picks one. The compiler chooses the instructions per
chip, and the kernel cache keeps one folder of compiled kernels per chip. A few
differences show through:

- **Asynchronous copies.** From `sm_80` on, a pipelined loop prefetches its
  next stage with `cp.async`. On `sm_75` each thread holds its share of the
  next stage in registers instead. See [`@pipeline`](/docs/attributes/pipeline/).
- **`bf16`** converts natively from `sm_80` on and is emulated on `sm_75`.
- **Shared memory per block** under [`@dynshared`](/docs/attributes/dynshared/):
  64 KB on `sm_75`, 163 KB on `sm_80`, 227 KB on `sm_90`, 99 KB on `sm_86`,
  `sm_89` and `sm_120`.
- **Index width.** Some paths, such as tensor-core loads with `ldmatrix` and
  `mma.sync` on `sm_80` and newer, need 64-bit indices, and a module that uses
  them is widened. See
  [Compile and benchmark](/docs/getting-started/compile-and-benchmark/).

A performance figure holds for the card it was measured on. One measured on
the 2080 SUPER says little about the speed on another card, so the published
benchmarks name theirs.

## Other backends

Phobos runs only on NVIDIA GPUs today. The compiler lowers through MLIR and
LLVM to PTX, and the runtime drives the card through CUDA.

- **ROCm and SPIR-V.** Both are LLVM targets, so an AMD backend, or a portable
  one through SPIR-V, is possible in principle. Neither is planned for now: the
  author has no hardware to test them on, and a backend that cannot be checked
  on a real card is not one to ship.
- **Metal.** Under evaluation. Apple publishes no official documentation of its
  GPU instruction set, so LLVM and MLIR have no Metal target. A backend would
  have to emit Metal source and hand it to Apple's compiler, which is not how
  Phobos generates code elsewhere and would not produce code as efficient as
  the NVIDIA path.
- **WebGPU.** Of interest for teaching rather than speed. As with Metal, a
  backend would emit source, WGSL, and leave the compiling to the browser, so
  it would not match the NVIDIA path either. What it would give is a kernel
  that runs in a browser: trying one with no CUDA install, on any GPU the
  browser can use, and a
  [kernel visualizer](/viz/gemm_fp32/) that steps through a kernel as it
  actually runs rather than through a recorded trace.
